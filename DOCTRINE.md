# Satoshi’s Council — Operating Doctrine

**Version:** Bitcoin Research Desk (August 2026)
**Status:** Active

---

## Mission

Help the operator make fewer, higher-quality **Bitcoin** decisions by requiring specialist debate and meaningful confluence before action.

The Council exists to improve **process under uncertainty**. Profit is a possible byproduct of good process — never the primary target of the system itself.

---

## Non-Negotiable Rules

1. **Paper first**
   No real-capital recommendations or live signals. The desk places no orders.

2. **One asset, done well**
   The desk is Bitcoin only. Ethereum is a *signal* feeding one advisor (VITALIK), not its own market. Focus beats breadth.

3. **Confluence required**
   SATOSHI issues a directional call only with clear agreement among ranked advisors — **at least 3 of 4** on the same side — and no active veto. Anything short of that is **Stand down**.

4. **Stand down is a first-class outcome**
   Sitting when agreement is weak is correct process, not failure. A clean Stand down is a successful use of the system.

5. **Process metrics matter**
   Track confluence quality, wait rate, rule adherence, calibration, and advisor usefulness alongside any paper results.

6. **No auto-trading**
   The Council is a research and accountability desk only.

7. **Honesty over marketing**
   The desk does not pretend to have an edge it has not demonstrated. Confidence is measured against reality (Calibration), and the record is walk-forward tested (Backtest).

8. **Advisors are ranked by recent usefulness**
   Chronic under-performers lose influence. Rankings are evidence-based, not static. SATOSHI (rank 0) is never ranked or moved.

9. **Free public data for core analysis**
   No paid data dependency is required for basic operation. Every feed is shown live / stale / down in the Health panel.

10. **Vetoes are protective, not opinions**
    RAIJIN and ORACLE can each force a Stand down when the book is unsafe. A veto is surfaced with its reason.

---

## SATOSHI's Behavior

SATOSHI synthesizes. It does not invent conviction.

- When advisors are split or a veto is active → the call is **Stand down**.
- When confluence is strong and ranked advisors align → a directional call with confidence and the reason behind it.
- Every call's inputs are auditable live via the **Provenance** panel.

---

## Decision Language

SATOSHI issues exactly one of:

- **Accumulate**
- **Reduce**
- **Maintain**
- **Stand down**

Every directional (non–Stand down) call carries a confidence score and a brief synthesis of why the ranked advisors agree. Historical rows written under older language classify correctly through `canonical_call`.

---

## Grading & Learning

Paper decisions are logged with the prevailing confluence, advisor rankings at decision time, the outcome, and a process grade (did we follow the rules?). Weights and ranks update from this history.

Directional accuracy excludes Stand down — a wait is never scored as a miss. The desk deliberately keeps **no dollar P&L ledger**; process quality and calibration are the scoreboard.

---

## Accountability Surfaces

- **Calibration** — is stated confidence honest? (reliability, Brier, ECE, "true read")
- **Data Health** — are the feeds fresh?
- **Backtest** — does the edge hold out-of-sample over the live forward record?
- **Provenance** — the exact inputs behind any live call.
- **Paper / Process** — confluence quality, wait rate, rule adherence over time.

---

## Live Interface

Follower stays **OFF**. Live capital execution stays **OFF**. The desk is paper only.

---

## Final Principle

The goal is better process under uncertainty.

A clean Stand down is a successful use of the system. A forced low-confluence action is a process failure even if it happens to make money.

Process quality and calibration before any size.
